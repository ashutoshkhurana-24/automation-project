Pravita’s Apartment

Say what you want, and the house does it.

Press the button on your phone and speak normally. It answers out loud, and nothing you say can break anything.

Say it like this

Three things, in this order — which room, what in it, what to do:

Ashu room ka fan chalu karo Master room ke cobs 40 kar do Living ka main curtain khol do

Extra words in between are ignored, so there is no need to talk like a machine. These do exactly the same thing:

Zara ashu ka fan on kar dijiye Ashu fan on

Short names work too — ashu becomes Ashu Room, foot becomes foot light. If a short name fits two things, the house asks which one instead of guessing.

Worth knowing

Say the names in English. Hindi verbs are fine — chalu karo, band kar do, khol do — but the name of the room and the thing should be the English one listed below. Those are the installer's own labels, and they are the names the house knows.

Two exceptions, because everybody says them: pankha works for a fan and parda works for a curtain. So Ashu ka pankha chalu karo is fine. Anything else needs the English word — batti will not work, light or cob will.

Where a room has two curtains it asks which, and where it has none it says so rather than switching on something with a similar name.

Dimming and colour

  • A number is brightness, out of a hundred: Ashu cobs 40
  • up and down nudge it from where it is now: Ashu cobs down
  • For colour, say the word — warm, cool, warmer, cooler — or both at once: Master cobs 40 warm

Only the ceiling cobs and a few lamps change colour; ask a plain light for colour and it says so rather than ignoring you. It names colours in its own words, so asking for warm may be confirmed as "set to candle" — that is the same thing, not a mistake.

A whole room at once

Living off Ashu lights off Ashu cobs on

The first is everything in the room. The second is only the lights, so a fan keeps running. The third is the whole ceiling together.

One kind, everywhere in the house

Saare AC band kardo Saare pankhe band karo Turn off all the lights Saare parde band kar do

Four kinds can be reached across the whole house at once — the lights, the fans, the curtains and the air conditioners. Each one touches only that kind: switching off every light leaves the fans running.

It will not guess at the whole house

Ask for a kind it does not have that name for and it says so, rather than switching off everything to be helpful. Say Sab band kar do when you really do mean the whole house.

You can ask, too

The same button answers questions, and a question never switches anything on or off by accident:

Kya chalu hai? Living mein kya chal raha hai? Ashu ka pankha on hai? Ashu mein kuch chalu hai?

Ask about one thing and you get a yes or a no — "Yes. Fan in Ashu Room is on." Ask what is on and you get the list. Where only some of a set is lit it says so: "Partly. 3 of the 11 cobs in Living are on."

Two things it cannot check

The air conditioners and the projector work over infrared, like an ordinary remote: the house can send to them but never hear back. So it says "the hub last sent AC on, but can't check it" — and if somebody used the AC's own remote, the house does not know. A curtain never reports its position either.

Televisions and the home theatre sound are checked properly. If it says the TV is on, it is on.

Said it by mistake?

Say any of these and your last command is put back:

Cancel Wapas karo Nahi nahi Galat Pehle jaisa karo

Only your last command, not anybody else's — and only one step back, within five minutes. After that it says "that was too long ago to put back safely", because the house has probably moved on.

Two it cannot put back perfectly

A curtain does not report its position, so cancel sends the opposite command: opened becomes closed. It tells you "this isn't exactly where it was". If the curtain had been half open, it will not go back to half open.

A television that was switched off. Volume and mute go back exactly, but switching a set back on lands it on the home screen rather than on whatever was playing — so it leaves it alone and says why.

If it keeps mishearing "cobs"

Say direct lights instead. It means exactly the same ceiling lights, and the phone gets it right first time because they are ordinary words.

Ashu ke direct lights on karo Living ke direct lights 40 karo

The other lights in a room — rope, profile, hanging, spot — are the indirect lights, and they work the same way.

Master ke indirect lights band karo
Both names still work

cobs has not gone anywhere. Say whichever comes out — and direct on its own is enough, you do not need the word lights.

Harshit Room has only one ceiling light, so it is named cob 1 there rather than being a group.

Going to bed

Say good night and your own room goes to sleep. Nobody else's.

Good night Shubh ratri So raha hoon Sone ja raha hoon

The lights go off. The fan and the AC keep running — the point is to fall asleep, not to be woken at two by a room gone still and warm.

It knows whose room from the phone

Each phone is set up with a name, so the same two words put a different room to bed. Mum and Dad put Master Room to bed, Ashu puts Ashu Room, Bhai puts Harshit Room.

On a phone that has not been set up it says "I don't know whose good night that is" and switches nothing off, rather than guessing at somebody's room.

Two things it does not do

It does not switch the television off. Say TV band karo as well if it is on.

It is one room, never the house. To put a different room to bed, name it the ordinary way: Dining off

Said it by accident? Cancel puts the whole room back, the same as any other command.

If something does not work

The left column is the English the phone says back. Match what you heard.

It saysWhat to do
I didn't catch thatIt heard nothing at all. Hold the button a moment longer, then speak.
I don't know that roomCheck the room name above — try just the first word.
I can't find that in …It found the room but not the thing. Open that room above for the right name.
… could be more than oneThe short name fits two things. Say a little more of it.
There is more than one curtain in …Say which one — it names them for you.
There is no fan in …That room has none. Open it above to see what it does have.
A curtain can only open, close or stopA curtain has no on or off — open it, close it, or stop it halfway.
… can't change colourThat one is a plain light. Give it a brightness, or just on.
I don't know whose good night that isThis phone has no name set, so it cannot tell which room to put to bed. Tell Ashutosh.
You haven't said anything I can cancelNothing to put back — either five minutes have passed, or that command came from a different phone.
The hub didn't answerThe controller is not responding. Try once more, and tell Ashutosh if it keeps happening.
Nothing at allThe phone has to be on the home Wi‑Fi. None of this works from outside the house.

You cannot break anything. The worst that happens is a light going on or off in some room, and saying the opposite puts it right. If you get it wrong, cancel is enough. Speak without worrying.
